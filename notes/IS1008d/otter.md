## Summary
The team met to finalize the detailed design, budget, and market strategy for the IS1008d remote control prototype.

### Product Design
Agni presented the final design for the remote control, which features an ergonomic wooden case with a customizable flip panel and an integrated yellow LCD screen. The device includes specific controls such as a large yellow on-off button, red volume toggles, green channel buttons, and a dark blue numeric pad.

Agni and Christine discussed technical placement for hardware components:
- The microphone location is debated; while Speaker 1 suggested placing it under the flip panel, Agni and Speaker 5 argued for side placement to ensure voice recognition works even when the device is closed.
- The weight and button shapes (egg-shaped) require further refinement to ensure the product feels substantial and high-quality.

### Financial Analysis
Speaker 1 reported that the current production cost is 9.95 euro, which is below the initial 12.50 euro budget. However, adding the LCD display will increase costs by approximately 2 to 3 euro per unit, bringing the total near the target limit.

Speaker 5 raised concerns regarding total profitability and market penetration:
- To achieve a 50 million pound profit, the company must capture 25% of a market estimated at 8 to 9 million units annually.
- This requires selling 2 million units at a price of roughly 50 euro per unit.
- Current calculations only account for production costs and do not yet include advertising, transportation, or labor.

### Market Strategy
Ed proposed shifting from traditional retail to an online "drop ship" model via platforms like Amazon or eBay. This strategy aims to reduce storage and point-of-sale transportation costs while leveraging the highly customizable nature of the product through digital imagery and video demonstrations.

The team discussed timing and testing:
- Agni expressed concern regarding the lack of time allocated for redesigning based on user feedback.
- Ed suggested synchronizing the launch with major sporting events, such as the World Cup, to maximize demand for hands-free voice control.
- A product evaluation phase will take between two to four weeks.
- Will the company be able to accommodate a second generation of the product if user testing necessitates significant changes?

## Outline
### Prototype Presentation and Design Features
- Speaker 4 presented a prototype of an ergonomic wooden remote control with a customizable color through staining.
- The design includes a flip panel revealing a yellow LCD display, volume toggles, channel controls, and a numeric pad.
- Key functional features include menu access on the right, voice recognition toggle on the left, and a customized backing.
- The team discussed placing the microphone on the side of the device rather than under the flip panel to ensure accessibility while closed.

### Financial Analysis and Budgeting
- Speaker 1 reported that current production costs are approximately 9.95 euro per unit, which is below the initial 12.50 euro budget.
- Including an LCD display could add 2 to 3 euro per unit, bringing total costs close to the target budget limit.
- Speaker 5 cautioned that production costs do not yet account for marketing, advertising, or transportation expenses required to reach a 50 million pound profit goal.
- Speaker 1 plans to negotiate with vendors to further reduce production costs to allocate more funds toward marketing and promotions.

### Market Feasibility and Sales Strategy
- An independent study suggests an annual market capacity of approximately 8 million units for this product type.
- To reach an ambitious goal of 2 million units in the first year, the company must capture about 25% of the market share.
- Speaker 2 proposed using internet sales channels like Amazon or eBay to enable drop shipping and minimize storage and retail transport costs.
- The team noted that online platforms are ideal for showcasing high customization options through digital imagery and videos.

### Product Evaluation and Launch Planning
- The team agreed to conduct product evaluation with real users over a period of two to four weeks before starting full production.
- Speaker 4 expressed concern regarding lack of redesign time if user feedback requires significant changes to the first generation model.
- Speaker 1 decided that no further major design changes will be made for this model; instead, customer feedback will inform future second-generation products.
- Speaker 2 suggested timing the product launch around major sports events or movie releases to maximize market impact and promotional synergy.
