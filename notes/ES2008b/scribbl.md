## Summary - Functional design meeting for a television remote
- The project lead and three team members reviewed a television remote-control design.
- Market research and three design presentations shaped the product goals.
- The group favored simplicity, fashion, and usable essential controls.
- The target demographic remains unsettled, and voice recognition was not favored.
- The project lead will add the minutes and slides to project documentation.

## Market research and design presentations
- The research and presentations pointed toward a remote that looks appealing, keeps common controls simple, and balances familiar design with useful features.
- The 100-person study found strong interest in appearance and simpler controls. [04:16]
  - 75% said remotes were ugly; 80% would pay more for a fancier-looking one.
  - More than half cited losing remotes as a major frustration.
  - People use about 10% of available buttons; channel and volume changes dominate.
  - Voice-recognition interest fell with age, while learning difficulty and repetitive strain also came up.
- The technical-functions presentation recommended balancing conventional controls with distinctive design. [09:23]
  - Prototype user interfaces could be tested for feedback.
  - The design must balance technical limits and price.
  - A fully featured remote is harder to learn; a basic remote omits functions.
- The working-design presentation mapped the remote's key components and needs. [15:20]
  - The proposed system includes an energy source, user interface, chip, and infrared bulb.
  - The presenter favored infrared transmission.
  - The design needs a long-lasting energy source, straightforward interface, capable chip, and reliable signal.
- The team connected good looks with a possible selling point for the remote. [14:21]

## Television-only scope and brand requirements
- The project lead narrowed the product to television use, excluded teletext, and kept the company's recognizable colors and slogan in scope.
- The group will not design for teletext because the internet has taken its place. [21:07]
- The product will focus on television rather than video and other uses. [22:30]
- The remote is expected to sell separately for €25, with yellow and black and the slogan “We put the fashion in electronics” informing its look. [24:50]

## Target market and feature trade-offs
- The group did not settle on an age-defined target market; the €12.50 production ceiling and simplicity goals weighed against voice recognition and other complexity.
- The €12.50 production limit made voice recognition seem costly for a €25 remote. [25:43]
  - The research's age breakdown chiefly concerned voice recognition.
  - The team questioned whether voice recognition was needed in a remote.
- A concealed set of secondary buttons could keep the main interface simple without requiring two remotes. [27:16]
  - One suggestion was a sliding panel or hatch for less-used controls.
  - The project lead noted that the design must not interfere with chip transmission.
- The group named simplicity, fashion, and usability as project priorities. [28:30]
- The target demographic remained open, with participants considering both broad age appeal and purchasing capacity. [29:33]
  - Suggestions ranged from ages 15–35 to under 65 and 20–55.
  - The group considered defining the audience by needs or spending ability rather than age alone.
- The team agreed that a lost-remote finding function would address a frequently reported frustration. [32:02]

## Core functions and battery ideas
- The group settled on prominent essential controls, less-prominent extra functions, and an attractive appearance; battery life and charging remained design considerations.
- The agreed functions included preventing loss, large accessible essential buttons, and access to extra functions. [32:25]
  - Extra controls should be less prominent or tucked away.
  - Fashion mattered so the remote would not be an eyesore on a coffee table.
- The group raised long battery life and a rechargeable cradle as possibilities. [33:47]
  - A cradle might help keep the remote in one place.
  - The project lead noted that living-room space should be considered.
