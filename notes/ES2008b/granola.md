## Market Research Findings
- 75% of 100 subjects found remotes ugly; 80% willing to pay more for a better-looking one
- Desire for voice recognition inversely related to age: 15-35 age group most interested, 60+ not willing to pay extra
- Top frustrations:
  - Losing the remote (50%+ cited as biggest issue)
  - Too many buttons, steep learning curve
  - Repetitive strain injury (RSI) from heavy use
- Users only use 10% of available buttons; most common actions are channel and volume changes (channel-zapping ~65% of use time)

## Design Direction
- Core emphasis: simplicity and fashion (“put the fashion in electronics”)
- Target group: broad appeal, roughly under 65; age segmentation less useful than lifestyle/attitude targeting
  - Voice recognition ruled out: too expensive, not essential for a remote
- Key design principles:
  - Large, prominent buttons for essentials (volume, channel)
  - Secondary/advanced functions hidden or less prominent, possibly behind a sliding panel
  - Ergonomic, no RSI risk
  - Fashionable enough to leave on a coffee table without being an eyesore
- Corporate identity: yellow and black color scheme, emphasis on the Rs in branding, must be visible in final design
- LCD screen floated as an option, no user preference data yet

## Technical and Working Design
- Remote is a communication device: user input → chip → infrared signal → TV receiver
- Four core components:
  1. Energy source (battery or rechargeable cradle)
  2. Chip (converts input to data)
  3. User interface
  4. Infrared bulb (transmits signal to receiver)
- Infrared chosen over alternatives: no line-of-sight required
- Chip quality critical for clean signal transmission
- Rechargeable cradle (like a phone dock) proposed as alternative to disposable batteries
  - Could also reduce remote-losing problem if it has a fixed home
