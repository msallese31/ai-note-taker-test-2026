## Summary
The team held a functional design meeting to establish core requirements and design philosophies for a new television remote control project.

### Design Objectives
Speaker 3 presented market research findings indicating that 75% of subjects find remotes unattractive, while 80% would pay more for a high-end aesthetic. The research also highlighted user frustrations regarding lost remotes and repetitive strain injury (RSI).
- Core Focus: Prioritize volume and channel changes, as users only utilize roughly 10% of available buttons.
- Functionality: Implement large, accessible buttons for essential tasks while keeping secondary settings less prominent.
- Aesthetics: Emphasize "fashion" and "simplicity" to ensure the device is not an eyesore on coffee tables.

### Technical Specifications
Speaker 5 discussed the technical interface, noting the need to balance conventional button placement with novel features like potential LCD screens or sliding hatches to hide complex functions. Speaker 4 outlined the working design schematic involving an energy source, a chip for data conversion, and an infrared bulb for transmission.
- Communication: Infrared was selected as the preferred method for projecting information to the receiver.
- Hardware Constraints: A production cost limit of 12.50 euros per unit must be respected to maintain a retail price of approximately 25 euros.
- Deprioritized Features: Management decided against including teletext support or voice recognition due to complexity, cost, and changing consumer habits.

### Project Direction
The group discussed defining a target demographic, weighing the benefits of targeting younger users (ages 15–35) interested in voice recognition versus a broader age range motivated by simplicity and ease of use. Speaker 2 emphasized incorporating the corporate identity—yellow and black colors with specific branding—into the final product.
- Potential Innovations: Consider a charging cradle or station instead of standard batteries to assist with both power longevity and preventing loss within the living room.
- Unresolved Questions: Would adding a "find my remote" beep function fit within budget? Could we implement a rechargeable battery system that doubles as a storage solution? Is it feasible to include advanced features without exceeding the production cost limit?

## Outline
### Meeting Introduction and Objectives
- Speaker 2 opened the functional design meeting at 12:13 with a 40-minute agenda.
- The agenda includes reviewing minutes, discussing general objectives, three presentations, new project requirements, and deciding on remote control functions.
- Functional design objectives were defined as fulfilling user requirements, technical function design, and working design.

### Market Research and User Needs
- Speaker 3 presented market research from Real Reaction involving 100 subjects in a usability lab.
- Findings showed that 75% of subjects found remotes ugly, while 80% would pay more for a "fancier" looking device.
- Users expressed frustration with losing remotes, difficulty learning complex interfaces, and repetitive strain injury (RSI).
- Data indicated that users only utilize about 10% of available buttons, primarily focusing on volume and channel changes.

### Technical Function Design
- Speaker 5 discussed the technical aspects of designing a communication device that sends messages to a television.
- The presentation emphasized the need for prototypes to gather feedback on subjective user experiences.
- A comparison was made between engineering-based designs with many features and basic designs with essential functions only.
- The goal is to find a balance between technological possibilities and economic constraints like production costs.

### Working Design and Components
- Speaker 4 explained the working design process by breaking down how energy is converted into practical functions.
- The proposed schematic includes four main components: an energy source, a chip to convert data, a user interface, and an infrared bulb.
- Infrared was identified as the preferred method for projecting information to the receiver.
- Key requirements for these components include a long-lasting energy source and a reliable infrared signal.

### New Project Requirements
- Speaker 2 announced that the team will not include teletext functionality as it has been replaced by the internet.
- The project scope is strictly limited to television remotes to avoid unnecessary complexity.
- The design must incorporate the corporate image, specifically using yellow and black colors and the company slogan.

### Target Group and Core Functions
- The team discussed targeting a demographic of teenagers and families or those under age 65.
- Voice recognition was largely dismissed due to high production costs and potential complexity for the target user.
- The group decided on core design principles of simplicity, fashion, and usability.
- Agreed functions include large buttons for essentials, hidden secondary functions, and a way to locate the remote if lost.
- The team also considered using a charging cradle instead of traditional batteries to improve convenience and location tracking.
