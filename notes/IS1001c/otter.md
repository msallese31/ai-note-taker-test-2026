## Summary
The team met to discuss the concept design for the IS1001c remote control, focusing on industrial design, user interface, and market trends.

### Design Concepts
ID presented research on standard components, noting that while push buttons are inexpensive, a wheel sensor is significantly more costly—approximately 50 times the price of a button. Despite this, the team decided to move forward with a wheel control to provide a competitive advantage and enhance innovation. UIZ suggested a simplified interface by removing unnecessary buttons and focusing on central controls like volume and channel adjustment. There was an initial discussion regarding protecting channel buttons with plastic covers; however, UIZ advised against this as it might hinder usability.

### Product Specifications
The group integrated market research provided by the expert which suggested "fancy" looks and "spongy" materials as current trends.
- Form Factor: The team reached a consensus on a banana-shaped design, which is ergonomic and fits the company's color scheme.
- Materials: The exterior will feature a spongy material for robustness and tactile appeal.
- Features: The remote will include backlit buttons that activate upon movement to assist in dark environments, though concerns regarding battery consumption remain.
- Technical Components: The build will utilize standard batteries and chip-on-print technology within an internal structure designed to support the spongy case.

### Unresolved Questions
Several technical and design points require further investigation before prototyping:
- Can the existing voice recognition chip from the manufacturing division be adapted for simple command recognition?
- Will adding motion sensors to trigger backlighting exceed the target production cost of 12 euros per unit?
- How can the internal structure be designed so that spongy materials do not interfere with consistent button presses?

## Outline

- Speaker 1 outlines the meeting's purpose to review investigations, decide on a concept, and define future tasks.
- The team recalls previous decisions to prioritize a simple interface with volume, power, and channel controls.
- Earlier agreements included protecting certain buttons with covers and adding a lighting feature for visibility.

### Industrial Design Investigation
- Speaker 3 presents research on standard remote control components like chips and push buttons.
- A significant concern is raised regarding the high cost of wheel sensors, which are fifty times more expensive than push buttons.
- Speaker 1 notes that the total production budget for the remote control is capped at 12 euros.
- Speaker 2 suggests exploring existing voice recognition technology from the manufacturing division to potentially add command features later.

### User Interface Design Discussion
- Speaker 2 proposes a simplified UI based on existing remote designs by removing unnecessary buttons.
- The design will focus on a central area containing big volume and channel controls for better usability.
- The team discusses rejecting the plastic cover idea for buttons to avoid breakage and difficulty in use.
- The group considers using a single wheel that can also be pushed down to serve multiple functions.

### Market Trends and Concept Refinement
- Speaker 4 presents market trends suggesting that products should have a fancy look, be innovative, and remain easy to use.
- Current fashion trends suggest utilizing "spongy" materials and organic fruit or vegetable shapes.
- The team brainstorms design ideas, ultimately deciding on a yellow banana shape because it is ergonomic and fits company colors.
- A spongy material is considered ideal because it provides robustness while following current aesthetic trends.

### Final Concept Decisions and Next Steps
- The final concept combines a banana-shaped spongy casing with an arched internal structure for component mounting.
- Decisions were made to include lighted buttons and a top-mounted wheel to improve both innovation and ease of use for all users.
- Speaker 1 instructs the industrial designer and UI designer to collaborate on developing the prototype's look and feel.
- Future tasks involve evaluating if the new design meets all user requirements before the next meeting.
