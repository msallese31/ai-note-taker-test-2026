## Summary - Remote-control concept design meeting
- The group met to review remote-control concepts and decide a direction.
- A standard wheel sensor costs 50 times as much as a push button.
- The group chose a yellow, banana-shaped remote with a wheel on top.
- The wheel stays despite its higher cost, for ease of use and innovation.
- The industrial and UI designers will work together on the next design and prototype.

## Wheel cost and control choices
- The industrial designer flagged the wheel sensor's cost, but the group kept the wheel as a possible differentiator.
- The earlier concept kept the remote simple and low-cost. [00:31]
  - It had a channel wheel, preview button, and protected channel-number buttons.
  - It also had volume, power, and a light to help find it.
  - LCD and voice features were excluded.
- A standard wheel sensor costs 50 times as much as a push button. [04:11]
  - The industrial designer asked whether the wheel was necessary.
  - The target production cost for the whole remote was up to €12. [04:17]
- The group questioned whether motion-activated lighting justified another sensor. [06:14]
  - The designer also considered continuous lighting when the remote is switched on.
  - The group noted that continuous light could consume battery power. [07:46]
- The UI designer favored a simple layout with large volume and channel controls. [08:16]
  - Some buttons on existing remotes were hard to reach with a thumb.
  - A plastic cover could make channel buttons harder to press. [10:02]
  - The wheel could be pressed down to recall a channel. [10:42]
- The group kept the wheel for its ease of use and potential to distinguish the product. [11:04]
  - The marketing expert was expected to weigh in on the wheel's value. [11:25]
- A manufacturing division email described an existing voice-recognition chip. [04:48]
  - It was currently used to record answers to specific questions.
  - The group thought it might be adapted to recognize commands.
  - No decision was made to add voice features.

## A banana-shaped remote
- The market-trend presentation prompted the group to choose a distinctive fruit-shaped design, settling on a banana.
- The market presentation ranked fancy look and feel first, innovation second, and ease of use third. [12:10]
  - It reported a trend toward fruit and vegetable forms.
  - A spongy material was suggested.
- The group considered pear and banana shapes, then favored the banana. [14:38]
  - The banana's curve could place the wheel on top and buttons along the side.
  - Its yellow color could make the remote easier to find.
  - The UI designer noted that yellow fit the company's color scheme. [17:24]
- The group wanted to retain the wheel as an innovative, easy-to-use feature. [14:21]

## Banana case and prototype work
- The group settled the main concept and asked the industrial and UI designers to develop it together for a prototype.
- The chosen concept was a yellow, banana-shaped remote with a wheel on top and buttons on the side. [21:24]
  - The case would have a spongy cover over an inner structure.
  - The design would use a standard battery and the selected chip-on-print component. [18:22]
- The group considered whether the controls would work for both left- and right-handed users. [19:45]
  - The UI designer raised concern that turning the remote could hide the buttons.
  - The group did not document a detailed resolution.
- The industrial designer and UI designer were asked to work together on the prototype. [21:24]
  - The next work included final look-and-feel design, UI design, and checking the product against user requirements.
  - Further instructions were to be sent by email.
